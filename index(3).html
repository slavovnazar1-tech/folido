❧

The editor's desk this week is cluttered with books. Not unusual, you might say — but books about the very act of reading: how we read, why we stop, what we lose when we skim. Alberto Manguel's A History of Reading sits open beside Maryanne Wolf's Reader, Come Home, and between them they suggest something troubling: that the way we read is changing our minds.

We scroll where we once lingered. We scan where we once savoured. The great fear is not that we will stop reading — we read more than ever — but that we will lose the capacity for deep, sustained attention. For the kind of reading that changes us, not merely informs us.

And yet. There are signs of a counter-movement. Book clubs are thriving. Bookshop membership is rising. The physical book is making a quiet comeback among the young. Perhaps we are not losing the thread after all — merely rediscovering it.

— Eleanor Ashworth, Editor
